I built a Ruby backtest (using DhanHQ API, an Indian brokerage) for a naked ATM index-option buying strategy on NIFTY and SENSEX. Methodology: daily SMA20/50 + RSI14 for trend regime (bullish/bearish/neutral, computed on expanding no-lookahead window); 15-minute intraday breakout of first candle's high/low OR RSI(14) cross above 60 / below 40 as entry trigger, direction filtered by regime; ATM CE bought on bull signal, ATM PE on bear signal; exit on -20% premium stop-loss or +40% premium target or end-of-day, using REAL historical ATM option premium data (Dhan's ExpiredOptionsData API, minute-level, ATM-relative strikes) not synthetic/approximated premiums. Ran over 180 calendar days (Jan-Jul 2026), ~241 trading-day datasets across both underlyings. Then ran a 200-combination offline grid search varying: stop-loss % (15/20/25/30/40), target % (20/30/40/60/80), entry logic (breakout-OR-rsi, breakout-only, rsi-only, breakout-AND-rsi), and regime-filter (on/off). Result: ZERO of 200 combinations were profitable. Best combo (breakout-AND-rsi, regime-filter on, SL=15%, target=20%) still lost -439% cumulative over 82 trades with 28% win rate. Tighter targets (20%) beat wider targets; requiring BOTH breakout AND RSI confirmation beat OR/single-condition entries; regime filter helped somewhat but not enough. Question for the council: (1) Is this a sound conclusion that naked ATM index-option buying with hard %-based SL/target on 15m signals is structurally negative-edge, or are there methodology flaws that could be producing an artificially pessimistic result? (2) What would you try next to find a genuinely profitable variant, if anything — e.g. underlying-price-based stops instead of premium-%, multi-day swing holds, IV-based filters, selling instead of buying, wider/narrower strike selection? Be specific and technical.
